## Core Idea

Build a reliable, unified directory of resources for unhoused people in Ashland. A scheduled AI agent could gather provider announcements from a dedicated inbox and public websites, compare them with existing information, and flag updates or contradictions for verification. The group saw potential for other communities to use the same approach.

## Proposed Workflow

- Subscribe a dedicated email address to OHRA, Southern Oregon Jobs with Justice, ACCESS, and other local provider newsletters.
- Run an agent on an agreed schedule to review incoming messages and relevant websites.
- Extract changes to services, hours, locations, eligibility, contacts, events, and availability.
- Cross-check updates against provider sources and flag conflicting or outdated details.
- Maintain one directory that can feed the public website. Human review versus automatic publishing remains undecided.

## 211 Exploration and User Experience

The group explored 211 as an existing resource service and possible information source. A participant recalled overlapping listings but felt the coverage was incomplete. This suggests an opportunity to improve local completeness and ease of access.

- Participants tried the phone menu and housing search. The call felt slow, and they noted that live help has operating hours.
- On iPad, the search instructions referred to a dropdown that was initially hard to find. A dropdown appeared after further interaction near the end of the transcript. Retest this as a usability issue; device incompatibility was not confirmed.
- "Housing-related coordinated entry" was unclear to participants. Public-facing listings should explain provider terminology in plain language.
- Calls, texts, and online search were considered as possible agent inputs. No integration method was selected.
